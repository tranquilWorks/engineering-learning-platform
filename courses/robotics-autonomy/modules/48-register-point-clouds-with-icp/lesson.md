# Iterate Planar Point-Cloud Registration with Outlier Gating



## Model, derivation, and conventions

`nearest_i=argmin_j ||R*source_i+t-target_j||`

`delta_R,delta_t=argmin sum_accepted ||delta_R*moved_i+delta_t-target_nearest_i||²`

`R_next=delta_R*R; t_next=delta_R*t+delta_t`

The source contains 120 points sampled from an asymmetric planar shape. Its radius varies with angular harmonics, its horizontal and vertical scales differ, and an additional horizontal term breaks simple symmetry. The target is generated by a true rotation of 0.18 rad and translation [0.25,-0.12] m, with small deterministic coordinate perturbations. The outlier control replaces a selected fraction of source points with distant clutter. Putting clutter in the source makes it participate in the nearest-neighbour query; merely adding far target points might leave all original nearest matches unchanged and produce an uninformative fault comparison.

The estimator starts with identity rotation and translation [selected_offset,0] m. That is an actual initial transform, not an error penalty added to the final result. Each iteration applies the current transform to every source point and finds its nearest target point. Production uses a spatial tree; the independent reference explicitly compares all squared distances. Correspondences are recomputed after movement, so this is an executed iterative closest-point procedure rather than fitting a known index pairing.

Normal mode accepts correspondences with distance at most 0.5 m. At least three accepted pairs are required by the declared implementation policy. Their transformed source and target coordinates are centred, and a two-dimensional rigid least-squares increment is fitted by singular-value decomposition. A determinant correction excludes reflection. The new rotation is the incremental rotation multiplied by the current rotation, and the new translation is incremental_rotation*current_translation plus incremental_translation. Adding translations without rotating the old one would apply the increment in the wrong frame.

The faulty comparison removes the distance gate and fits every nearest correspondence, including source clutter. It preserves the selected initial offset, outlier fraction, point clouds and iteration budget. Clutter can pull the fit toward a compromised transform even while the all-data residual falls. The ordinary ICP objective is a nearest-neighbour squared-error function with a local correspondence assignment, so reducing it need not recover the known synthetic transform. The normal gated fit likewise has a limited basin of attraction and can settle at a wrong local alignment.

The maximum budget is thirty actual updates. An update is considered converged when its translation norm is below 1e-9 m and its absolute rotation angle is below 1e-9 rad. Insufficient matches and reaching the maximum budget are retained as distinct statuses. The displayed iteration count is the number of executed updates; it is not computed from initial offset by a convenient formula. A maximum-budget result can still provide a finite residual and useful failure evidence without being declared converged.

The first metric is the final nearest-correspondence RMS among currently accepted pairs, in metres. The second measures the RMS physical displacement between the estimated and true transform applied to the entire clean shape. It therefore includes the effect of rotation through actual point positions while retaining units of metres. It does not add metres to radians in an arbitrary pose score. The third metric is the executed update count. Rotation and translation themselves, correspondence masks and objective history remain available for independent checks.

For the normal fit, the retained objective truncates each squared nearest distance at 0.5² m² before averaging. This makes rejected large residuals contribute a fixed cap and supports an interpretable descent check when the accepted set changes. The faulty fit retains the full mean squared nearest distance. Comparing those two objective values as though they were the same cost would be misleading; they measure different loss functions. Within either mode, the iteration history can establish descent while the independent truth-based transform error establishes whether that descent led to the intended geometry.

A simple composition check illustrates the frame issue. If the current translation is [1,0] m and the next increment rotates by ninety degrees with zero translation, the composed translation becomes [0,1] m. Keeping [1,0] would rotate the cloud around the wrong effective origin. The present fixture uses smaller rotations, but the same composition rule applies at every iteration. This is planar SE(2) registration with known synthetic evaluation truth, not full three-dimensional registration, a global solver or a guarantee that all accepted pairs represent the same physical surface.

## Recovery

Restore the gate, restart from the same initial transform and compare the final rigid transform, accepted correspondence set and executed iteration history.

Repeat a saved nominal setting and confirm that its values and curves return. Recovery must restore the governing mechanism and its evidence, not merely clear a warning.

## Alternative and limiting cases

The problem is planar SE(2), uses a fixed local initializer family and can reach a wrong local alignment or its iteration budget. Zero clutter can hide the rejection fault. A decreasing nearest-neighbour objective or tiny final increment does not establish the correct physical correspondence or a global optimum.

The reference independently constructs the two clouds, finds nearest neighbours by a full distance matrix and fits each planar increment using an atan2 cross/dot-product formula. ## Focused check and teach-back

Why can an ICP run converge with a small residual and still have a wrong transform?

Answer rationale: Nearest-neighbour assignments and rigid fitting optimize a local objective. A wrong correspondence pattern can be self-consistent and produce tiny updates; independent geometry, initialization limits and truth-based evaluation are separate checks.
