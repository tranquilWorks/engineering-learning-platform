# Capstone: perceive, grasp, and control a two-link robot

The final manipulation capstone carries one object estimate through frames, inverse kinematics, frictional grasp evaluation, contact-force control, passivity accounting, and timed-interface recovery. Each stage constrains the next. A precise arm endpoint does not compensate for a non-closing grasp; a stable contact force does not compensate for the wrong object frame; a successful pickup does not authorize success if the interface replay never recovered. The capstone passes only when all six requirement margins are nonnegative in the same trace.

## Model, derivation, and conventions

A camera observes a planar object. The base estimate uses `p_b^o=t_b^c+R_b^c p_c^o`, with deterministic position noise in centimetres. Broken mode omits this extrinsic. Analytic elbow-down inverse kinematics solves a two-link arm and forward kinematics produces the commanded endpoint. Physical pickup error compares that endpoint with the true transformed object, not with its own estimate.

The simplified grasp margin is `gamma=mu-0.22`, representing the friction remaining beyond the task's required cone slope. Contact is a spring with stiffness `600 N/m`. A delayed velocity controller drives force toward `10 N`. Nominal velocity and gains are bounded, while a passivity tank starts at `0.18 J` and pays positive contact work `max(Fv,0) Delta t`. If requested work exceeds available energy, velocity is scaled. Broken mode uses positive force feedback and bypasses tank enforcement.

Six normalized margins cover pickup tolerance, reach, closure, final force error, minimum energy, and recovered timed interfaces. The requirement trace names P46/P62, P29/P40, P61, P38/P39, and P66/P67 as contributing foundations.

## Predict before running

At baseline noise and friction, predict a small pickup error, positive reach, positive closure margin, final contact error below three newtons, nonnegative tank energy, and a recovered interface. Capstone success should be one. Increasing visual noise alone should reduce pose margin without changing contact dynamics. Reducing friction alone should reduce closure margin without moving the estimated target.

Broken integration should miss the object because it uses camera coordinates as base coordinates. It should also report negative closure interpretation, contact-force runaway from the wrong feedback sign, negative energy because passivity enforcement is absent, and failed interface recovery. Multiple violations matter: repairing only the pose cannot make the capstone pass.

## Baseline workflow

Run with `1 cm` visual noise and friction coefficient `0.6`. In the response plot, distinguish true object, estimated grasp, and interpolated endpoint approach. Confirm pickup error is below the `0.055 m` limit. The final pose is generated by the analytic two-link branch, while the independent true marker prevents a self-referential residual.

Inspect all six requirement margins. The first two cover perception and kinematics; the third covers grasp closure; the fourth covers final contact-force error; the fifth covers minimum passivity energy; and the sixth covers timed-interface recovery. Capstone success is their conjunction. The minimum-energy metric retains joules and should remain positive after the commanded contact work.

## Two one-variable sweeps

For sweep one, change only visual noise from one to four centimetres. Camera extrinsic, link lengths, friction, force gains, delay, tank, and interface recovery remain fixed. Pickup error grows and pose margin shrinks. The other requirement margins should remain essentially unchanged. This isolates perception robustness inside the integrated trace.

For sweep two, restore visual noise and reduce only contact friction from `0.6` to `0.3`. Pose and kinematics return to baseline. Closure margin falls from generous to narrow but remains positive because the task threshold is `0.22`. This illustrates a capstone near-limit that still passes; it does not claim a calibrated material coefficient or slip test.

## Intentionally broken case

Broken mode combines four named assumption failures. It skips the camera extrinsic, so IK precisely reaches the wrong point. It reverses the closure interpretation, so a unilateral grasp cannot be trusted. It changes negative contact feedback into positive feedback and omits energy limiting, causing penetration, force, and extracted tank energy to run away within finite clamps. It also bypasses timed-interface recovery.

All values remain bounded and serializable. A superficial test for finite output would pass. The signed requirements expose the integration failure: position error exceeds tolerance, closure is negative, force error exceeds three newtons, tank energy becomes negative, and the interface margin fails. The capstone therefore demonstrates why one final position metric is insufficient.

## Recovery

Recover in dependency order. Validate frames, units, and timestamps; transform the visual target into the base; reject unreachable or discontinuous IK; verify positive grasp closure before contact; apply negative force feedback with velocity bounds; debit positive contact work from the passivity tank; and hold or retreat when energy is exhausted. Replay the interface fault in source order and require a retained recovery event.

After each repair, recompute the entire requirement vector. Do not manually flip a success flag or ignore an earlier failure. If friction margin is nonpositive, choose different contacts or abort. If perception remains outside tolerance, reobserve. If energy reaches zero, stop injecting work. Recovery is an evidence-preserving state transition, not threshold relaxation.

## Alternative and limiting cases

Production manipulation might use six-degree-of-freedom pose estimation, collision-aware IK, polyhedral grasp wrench spaces, impedance or hybrid force control, torque sensing, and passivity observers with energy tanks at the actuator interface. Optimization can couple grasp selection and motion. This planar implementation keeps those mechanisms distinct but causally connected.

With perfect pose and ample friction, contact and interface requirements dominate. With friction exactly `0.22`, closure margin is zero and should be treated as fragile. A zero initial energy tank permits no positive contact work, so the correct response is hold or retreat. With zero communication delay, passivity risk falls but does not disappear under a wrong sign. If the target sits on the workspace boundary, reach can pass while manipulability collapses.

## Independent evidence and MATLAB-style design boundary

The independent reference reconstructs the camera transform, analytic IK and forward check, friction margin, delayed spring-contact simulation, tank accounting, timed-interface outcome, and six requirement margins. It imports no production experiment, consumes no production result, and perturbs no production value. Five scenario signatures retain cumulative success, pickup error, and minimum energy.

This is a deterministic software capstone. No MATLAB Robotics or Simscape model, camera, gripper, force sensor, browser accessibility review, learner trial, physical HIL, impact test, safety certification, release, deployment, credentials/settings, or production manipulation was executed. Passing software requirements does not certify safe contact.

## Engineering review checklist

- Trace perception evidence through the same frame chain used by IK.
- Compare endpoint with an independent true or measured object pose.
- Require positive unilateral grasp closure before force control.
- Verify feedback sign, saturation, delay, and environment units.
- Account for positive contact work in a nonnegative energy tank.
- Stop or retreat when any cumulative requirement cannot recover.
- Retain source-time interface faults and recovery transitions.
- Report every signed margin rather than only a final success bit.

## Common mistakes

Common failures use the estimated object as both command and truth, making pickup error identically zero. Others call any positive friction coefficient force closure, mix newtons and penetration metres, or use the wrong sign in contact feedback. Passivity tanks are sometimes logged but not enforced. A replay may show recovery even though the online executive never entered it. Capstones also fail educationally when they merely place prior plots side by side without shared state, causal handoffs, and one requirement decision.
