# Timestamp calibration, watchdog and measured recovery

The synthetic log supplies two exact synchronization pairs: local source time and remote-clock time. Their slope estimates the remote rate `a = Δt_remote/Δt_source`; corrected timestamps are `t_remote/a`. This calibration assumes zero offset and noiseless synchronization pairs. It is not a deployed clock-synchronization protocol. The residual plot deliberately retains the raw remote-minus-source discrepancy, so a corrected replay can coexist with a visible raw drift fault.

In healthy replay, propagate with corrected source intervals only while the watchdog is active. A gap strictly greater than 60 ms enters hold; the state then stops propagating until the next valid checkpoint resets the estimate and resumes. Recovery duration is the difference between the recorded resume and hold timestamps. For the 120 ms dropout beginning at 0.80 s, the last sample is at 0.78 s, hold starts at 0.86 s and the checkpoint at 0.92 s yields a measured 60 ms hold. This replaces the former assigned 20 ms constant. Review the actual events because grid rounding changes an off-grid dropout's first valid sample.

Broken replay uses jittered arrival ordering and arrival deltas with a weak measurement update. It never detects the hold/resume transition. Its displayed duration is explicitly censored: time from dropout onset to the end of the two-second record, accompanied by `recovery_observed=false`. It is not a measured successful recovery or a fabricated latency sentinel. No-dropout-sized gaps can leave healthy recovery untriggered and duration zero; explain why that differs from a detected and completed hold.

Sweep dropout duration at fixed clock drift and predict the hold interval from timestamps before running. Sweep drift at fixed dropout and distinguish raw clock residual, calibration scale, drift-review event and state error. Enable broken mode, identify the missing recovery event and compare state histories. Restore healthy mode and verify deterministic recovery. This is offline synthetic replay; source-time processing does not establish online network deadlines, transport loss handling, ROS middleware behavior or physical safety.
