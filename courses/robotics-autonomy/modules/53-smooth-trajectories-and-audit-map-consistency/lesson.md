# Smooth Trajectories and Audit Map Consistency

**Guiding question:** How can a smoother lower trajectory error while still failing a covariance or held-out map-consistency audit?

A filter estimates the present using past and current measurements. An offline smoother can revisit an earlier state after future measurements arrive. That extra information often reduces trajectory error, but a smoother is trustworthy only when its covariance follows the same backward recursion and when the result predicts data that were not used to fit it. A visually smooth path or a lower training residual is not enough.

This laboratory simulates one-dimensional robot position and velocity at 0.1 s intervals. A deterministic low-frequency acceleration produces the truth trajectory. Noisy absolute-position fixes are available, but every fifth fix is withheld from estimation and reserved for audit. A constant-velocity Kalman filter runs forward; the Rauch-Tung-Striebel (RTS) recursion runs backward. The result is judged by position RMSE against known simulation truth, covariance consistency, and error on the held-out fixes.

## Model, derivation, and conventions

The state is $x_k=[p_k,v_k]^T$, with metres and metres per second. The constant-velocity transition is

$$x_{k+1}=Fx_k+Gw_k,\quad F=\begin{bmatrix}1&\Delta t\\0&1\end{bmatrix},\quad G=\begin{bmatrix}\Delta t^2/2\\\Delta t\end{bmatrix}.$$

The process-noise slider is acceleration standard deviation in $\mathrm{m/s^2}$, so $Q=\sigma_a^2GG^T$ plus a tiny fixed numerical floor. Position fixes follow $z_k=Hx_k+v_k$ with $H=[1,0]$ and $R=\sigma_z^2$ in square metres. The forward filter stores predicted and filtered state/covariance pairs.

For $k=N-2$ down to zero, the RTS gain and recursion are

$$C_k=P_k^fF^T(P_{k+1}^-)^{-1},$$

$$x_k^s=x_k^f+C_k(x_{k+1}^s-x_{k+1}^-),$$

$$P_k^s=P_k^f+C_k(P_{k+1}^s-P_{k+1}^-)C_k^T.$$

The final smoothed state equals the final filtered state because no future information exists beyond it. For consistency, the lesson computes one-state normalized estimation error squared,

$$\epsilon_k=\frac{(p_k^s-p_k^{true})^2}{P_{k,pp}^s},$$

and reports the fraction below the 95-percent one-degree-of-freedom chi-square bound 3.841. Held-out RMSE compares smoothed position with every fifth absolute-position fix, none of which updated the filter.

## Predict before running

At the baseline process noise of $0.06\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$ and measurement noise of $0.15\ \mathrm{m}$, the smoother should reduce earlier errors and most normalized errors should remain below the displayed bound. Predict that the final point receives no backward correction.

Increasing process noise makes the model more willing to bend between measurements. In this deterministic simulation, the constant-velocity model omits a known acceleration, so a larger process-noise setting may improve RMSE by acknowledging model mismatch. That does not mean arbitrary process noise is beneficial; it trades model confidence for measurement responsiveness. Increasing measurement noise to 1 m should make the estimator trust fixes less, increase trajectory and held-out error, and broaden covariance.

## Baseline workflow

Run the defaults and inspect the position plot. Compare the estimated trajectory with truth over the entire eight-second interval, not only at the final state. Record smoothed position RMSE. Then inspect normalized squared error against 3.841. A few excursions can occur in a finite deterministic run; consistency coverage summarizes how often the covariance contains the actual error at the selected confidence level.

Finally record held-out position-fix RMSE. These fixes were generated by the same declared sensor model but were not used in either forward filtering or backward smoothing. The metric therefore checks predictive agreement rather than training fit. It is still not a fully independent field dataset, because truth and noise are deterministic parts of this reviewed simulation.

## Two one-variable sweeps

For Sweep 1, hold measurement noise at 0.15 m and change acceleration-noise standard deviation from 0.001 through 0.06 to 0.5 $\mathrm{m/s^2}$. At the low endpoint, the filter nearly insists on constant velocity, so unmodeled acceleration can create biased residuals and overconfidence. At the high endpoint, the trajectory may fit better and covariance may become conservative. Record RMSE and consistency separately; a conservative covariance can obtain high coverage without being precise.

For Sweep 2, restore process noise to 0.06 $\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and increase measurement noise from 0.01 through 0.15 to 1.0 m. Larger $R$ reduces Kalman gain, so estimates follow the process model more strongly. Held-out measurements are also noisier, so held-out RMSE contains both state error and withheld sensor noise. Explain this before comparing endpoints.

## Intentionally broken case

Broken mode publishes the causal filtered trajectory as though it were smoothed, then multiplies its covariance by 0.12 without applying a corresponding state update. The position curve can still look plausible. RMSE increases because future data never correct earlier states, while covariance becomes unjustifiably tight. Normalized errors therefore exceed the chi-square bound more often, and held-out error also degrades.

This failure represents pipelines that post-process covariance independently, reuse a visualization covariance, or label a filtered result as batch-optimized. The key invariant is coupling: every covariance contraction must follow from the same measurement or smoothing gain that changed the state. Confidence cannot be tuned after the fact merely to make a map look certain.

## Recovery

Recovery restores the full backward recursion for both state and covariance, uses the exact baseline noise assumptions, and reruns the held-out audit. Verify exact return of RMSE, consistency coverage, and held-out error. Increasing covariance until coverage passes would be mitigation, not recovery, because it would not restore the missing future-information update. Fitting the withheld samples would also invalidate the audit.

In a real map pipeline, recovery should preserve which factors belonged to optimization and which belonged to validation. Once held-out landmarks are used for tuning, they are no longer held out and a new validation set is required.

## Alternative and limiting cases

At the final time, $x_{N-1}^s=x_{N-1}^f$ and $P_{N-1}^s=P_{N-1}^f$. If future prediction and smoothing states agree exactly, the backward correction is zero. If process covariance tends to zero under a wrong constant-velocity model, estimates can become precise but biased. If measurement covariance tends to infinity, absolute fixes contribute almost no information and withheld error exposes model drift.

Batch least squares over all states is an alternative formulation. For linear Gaussian models it produces the same posterior mean as RTS smoothing when priors and covariances match. Factor-graph smoothing generalizes the same idea to nonlinear states and sparse factors. Agreement depends on using identical models, not on the solver name.

## Independent evidence and MATLAB-style design boundary

Independent scenario evidence evaluates the reviewed linear-Gaussian recursions and fixed noise sequence without importing the production experiment. Production evidence separately executes the catalog entrypoint. Baseline and recovery share inputs and must reproduce the same signature exactly; broken evidence is generated by the declared failure, not by perturbing a stored nominal result.

No MATLAB Kalman functions, real GNSS, motion capture, SLAM map, browser accessibility test, learner study, or physical HIL ran. Known simulation truth permits RMSE and NEES calculations that would not be directly available in deployment. The result does not establish real sensor covariance, nonlinear robustness, or safety.

## Engineering review checklist

- Keep predicted, filtered, and smoothed states and covariances distinct.
- Verify units in $F$, $G$, $Q$, $H$, and $R$.
- Ensure held-out fixes never enter filtering, smoothing, or tuning.
- Check final-time equality and zero-future-information limits.
- Evaluate precision, consistency, and held-out error separately.
- Trace every covariance change to the corresponding gain and state update.

## Common mistakes

- Calling a low-pass filtered curve a smoother.
- Running the backward state recursion but forgetting the covariance recursion.
- Shrinking covariance to match a desired confidence display.
- Reporting only training residual from factors already optimized.
- Treating high coverage from huge covariance as accurate estimation.
- Reusing held-out measurements during parameter tuning and still calling them validation.

## Focused check and teach-back

Derive why the last smoothed state equals the last filtered state. Explain how a future innovation changes an earlier state through $C_k$, and why covariance must change through the companion equation. Then compare baseline with broken mode using all three metrics, not plot smoothness alone. End with the boundary: deterministic one-dimensional Python smoothing and audit evidence only; MATLAB runtime, learner/accessibility validation, physical hardware/HIL, certification, release, deployment, and production use remain unperformed.
