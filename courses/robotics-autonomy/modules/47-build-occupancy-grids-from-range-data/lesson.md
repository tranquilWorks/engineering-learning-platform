# Trace Range Beams and Update an Occupancy Grid



## Model, derivation, and conventions

`log_odds_next=clip(log_odds+inverse_sensor_evidence,-5,5)`

`hit_evidence=log(p_hit/(1-p_hit)); traversed_free_evidence=-hit_evidence`

`entropy=sum(-p*log2(p)-(1-p)*log2(1-p)) over grid cells`

The map contains 31 by 31 square cells with 0.1 m sides and centre coordinates from -1.5 to 1.5 m. The range sensor lies at the centre of the middle cell. A declared synthetic world contains outer walls, a rectangular obstacle and a small circular obstacle, rasterized into occupied truth cells. Selected beams are uniformly spaced in angle with a fixed small angular offset and a maximum range of 1.4 m. Their measurements are generated by actual traversal through this grid, stopping at the first occupied cell rather than assigning a coverage percentage from beam count.

Production uses grid-boundary traversal to enumerate the cells crossed by each ray. A simultaneous horizontal and vertical boundary crossing advances both cell indices, so cells touched only at a corner do not acquire evidence. That tie convention is important: two nominally plausible ray tracers can otherwise disagree on a diagonal beam. The independent reference intersects the ray with each cell's axis-aligned slab, discards zero-length contacts, sorts positive intervals and finds the first occupied cell. Comparing whole cell paths checks the geometry before comparing a summary entropy.

Every cell begins at probability 0.5, corresponding to zero log odds. A ray contributes negative evidence to each crossed free cell and positive evidence to its first occupied endpoint. The hit-probability control specifies the positive evidence log(p_hit/(1-p_hit)); its negative is the free-space contribution. Thus the selected probability is a declared inverse-sensor-model strength. It is not a measured posterior probability for every endpoint and is not automatically a calibrated statement about a physical sensor.

Log odds are accumulated and clipped to the interval [-5,5]. Converting back through the logistic function gives the actual occupancy probabilities. The endpoint-only fault keeps the positive endpoint updates but omits all traversed free-cell updates. A crossed cell therefore remains at its 0.5 prior unless it receives other evidence. That is missing free-space information, not a fabricated occupied observation and not an invented false-free cell. The mechanism of failure must match the name of the reported metric.

The first metric is mean posterior occupancy over cells actually observed as hits. Repeated beams can hit the same cell and strengthen its accumulated confidence. The second metric sums Bernoulli entropy over all 961 map cells in bits. An untouched cell contributes one bit, whereas a confident cell contributes less. This is the sum of marginal entropies under the grid's simplifying independence model, not the joint uncertainty of an entire correlated physical world. Empty hit sets require an unavailable metric status rather than a perfect-looking mean.

The third metric is the fraction of all truly free map cells whose posterior remains at or above 0.45. It measures free space that the posterior has not established as free. Its denominator includes unobserved and occluded free cells, so it is a coverage-and-evidence measure rather than a false-positive rate restricted to visible cells. Those unknown cells are legitimate limitations of the finite rays, not evidence that the map should invent free space behind obstacles. In endpoint-only mode the free-cell probabilities stay at their prior and this fraction is one for this fixture.

For a single hit update with p_hit=0.72, the log-odds increment is approximately 0.944 and the updated probability is 0.72. A matching single free update produces probability 0.28. Two identical hit updates produce about 0.869 before clipping. That arithmetic explains how evidence accumulates, but it also exposes a limitation: repeated highly correlated rays are being counted as repeated evidence. More beams can lower the displayed entropy without furnishing the same amount of independent physical information.

The map heat image shows actual probabilities on axes measured in metres: white is near zero occupancy, black is near one, and grey is the unknown prior. Gold markers identify observed hit cells. A section through the grid compares probability with the declared truth geometry, allowing a learner to locate unknown regions and obstacle boundaries. Displaying a smooth artificial entropy curve alone would hide the ray and cell operations being taught. The finite grid, deterministic perfect range generation, fixed pose, hard occupancy boundary and saturation rule exclude range noise, pose uncertainty, dynamic obstacles and probabilistically calibrated sensor fusion. The exercise establishes a transparent mapping mechanism, not a field-ready occupancy estimator.

## Intentionally broken case

The mapper records occupied endpoints but skips the free cells that each ray actually traversed. It preserves beam geometry and evidence strength.

Run the same parameter values with the fault enabled. Compare complete curves as well as summary metrics. Identify the actual operation that changed and calculate why it affects the measured result. A changed warning label is not numerical evidence.

## Recovery

Restore free-cell evidence, reset the prior grid and replay the same beams. Check whole probability rows and ray paths as well as aggregate entropy.

Repeat a saved nominal setting and confirm that its values and curves return. Recovery must restore the governing mechanism and its evidence, not merely clear a warning.

## Alternative and limiting cases

Unobserved and occluded cells retain their prior; the unconfirmed-free fraction deliberately includes them and should not be read as a visible-cell false-positive rate. Known sensor pose, deterministic ranges, independent-cell updates and repeated-ray evidence are simplifying assumptions. Marginal entropy reduction does not prove calibrated joint uncertainty.

## Common mistakes

Do not infer correctness from a changing headline alone. Unobserved and occluded cells retain their prior; the unconfirmed-free fraction deliberately includes them and should not be read as a visible-cell false-positive rate.

Do not change both sliders at once and attribute the result to one cause. Separate a model assumption from a measured property, and a finite-horizon observation from a universal guarantee. Floating-point roundoff is not a physical effect; equally, an attractive plot is not a substitute for the governing calculation.

## Focused check and teach-back

Why should the endpoint-only fault be described as missing free-space evidence rather than creating false-free cells?

Answer rationale: Its omitted negative updates leave crossed free cells at the 0.5 prior. They remain unknown; the algorithm has not asserted that occupied cells are free. The metric must describe the posterior that was actually produced.
