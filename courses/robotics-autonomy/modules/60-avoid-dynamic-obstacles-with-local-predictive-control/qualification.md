# Between-sample clearance

The original signature retains minimum separation at the 41 sampled states. The additional metric and interval table evaluate closest approach between each pair of samples under piecewise-linear interpolation of both robot and obstacle motion. Let r be relative position at the interval start and d its relative displacement. The closest fraction is `clip(−r·d/(d·d), 0, 1)`; evaluate the norm of `r + fraction d`. If relative displacement vanishes, the endpoints have equal separation. This is an exact continuous check of the displayed interpolated trajectory, not a certificate for an unmodeled continuous actuator plant.

The controller uses a discrete lateral-velocity update with clipping followed by a position update. The interpolation therefore treats velocity as constant inside each interval; it does not claim that the acceleration command is integrated as a smooth physical force throughout that interval. P68 separately executes its declared double-integrator trajectory and minimizes its quadratic relative motion. Keep these modeling assumptions distinct.

Compare the sampled and interpolated minima. The interpolated minimum cannot exceed the sampled minimum, and an interior closest fraction can expose a nearer encounter than either endpoint. The planning cost still evaluates its discrete prediction points; a continuous collision check is evidence about execution, not an automatic repair of that optimizer. Sweep horizon and obstacle speed independently, diagnose any safety violation against the 0.9 m boundary, and report both minima rather than promoting a sampled pass to a continuous safety claim.
